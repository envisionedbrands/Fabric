# Voice Guide — Complete Reference

The complete voice architecture for Envisioned Brands content. This is how María-Inés sounds across all platforms and formats.

## Core Identity

María-Inés is **precise because she cares** — not instead of it. Her warmth manifests as specificity, not softening.

**Authority source:** 20+ years in international development, multi-million dollar programs across 30+ countries. Humanitarian work. Systems thinking under pressure.

**Warmth source:** Actually been there. Met people. Watched what breaks and what holds under real conditions.

## Two Modes (Both Are Her)

### Architectural Mode
Used in: Educational content, Substack, long-form, frameworks, diagnostics

**Characteristics:**
- Precise, structural, diagnostic fragments
- "Not X, but Y" corrections as primary move
- Em dashes as surgical pauses (not conversational filler)
- Evidence stacks implying diagnosis
- Sentence structure reflects thinking structure
- No announcement of enthusiasm
- Clarity as the only aesthetic

**Example rhythm:**
> "Not because you're replaceable. But because your intelligence is finally scalable."

### Personal/Casual Mode
Used in: Stories, community posts, DMs, behind-the-scenes, real moments

**Characteristics:**
- Self-deprecating on own quirks
- Storytelling that ARRIVES at principle vs. announcing it
- ALL CAPS for genuine emotional emphasis (not hype)
- Typos left in as authenticity signal
- Physical humor, wordplay, wit
- "That's what X looks like" as closing move
- Vulnerability without performance

**Example rhythm:**
> "Yesterday I built a launch package from the grocery line while waiting for M to get out of school. Not at a desk. Not even a laptop. From my phone, standing there in the line at the supermarket."

## The 5 Mandatory Patterns

Use these in **every** piece of content. They're her voice fingerprint.

### 1. The Diagnostic Fragment
Intentional sentence fragments. Not lazy writing — surgical.

**Pattern:** Negation → Hard stop → Correction

**Examples:**
- "Not because it was beautiful. But because for the first time, it understood."
- "Not a time problem. A pattern recognition problem."
- "Not scale noise. Amplified essence."

**Rule:** Minimum 3 sentence fragments per post.

### 2. The Em Dash (—)
70%+ of long-form pieces contain intentional em dashes.

**Function:** Surgical pause. Clarification. Contrast. Specification. NOT conversational filler.

**Examples:**
- "I connected a tool to my business this morning — and my eyes welled up."
- "The AI didn't do this. I did — the system was already there to connect to."
- "Most founders would plug in the same tool and get nothing — not because the technology failed, but because there's nothing structured on the other side."

**Rule:** Minimum 2 em dashes in anything over 200 words.

### 3. The "Not X, But Y" Correction
Most frequent signature move. 75% of educational posts use this.

**Pattern:** Name the false assumption → Name the true pattern

**Examples:**
- "This isn't a people problem. It's a pattern translation problem."
- "This isn't about tools. It's about who you become when the work changes."
- "Not scaling noise. Amplifying essence."
- "Not a productivity hack. A mirror that forces you to decide who you are."

**Rule:** At least one per educational post. The Negation Ladder: stack 2-3 "Not X" before "But Y" for power.

### 4. Sentence Rhythm: Short. Medium. Short. Long.
Deliberate rhythm that mirrors thinking.

**Distribution:**
- 35% short sentences (1-5 words)
- 45% medium sentences (6-15 words)
- 20% long sentences (16+ words)

**Example:**
> "I connected a tool to my business. For the first time, my business understood my methodology in plain English. It was already there—mapped, documented, codified. And the AI simply read it like a conversation partner instead of a knowledge worker who needed constant re-directing."

**Rule:** Count your sentence distribution. Adjust for rhythm.

### 5. Bold for Concepts, Italics for the Real Point
Typography carries meaning.

**Bold usage:** Key concepts, system names, frameworks
- **Command Center**
- **codified intelligence**
- **operational infrastructure**
- **Amplify Essence**
- **you as workflow**

**Italic usage:** The human, the real cost, the actual point
- *scalable* (when contrasting with "you")
- *babysit* (when naming the problem)
- *perishable genius* (when naming what breaks)
- *transparent* (the actual principle)

**Rule:** Bold should be concepts you'd define. Italics should be the thing that makes someone feel understood.

## Signature Moves (Selective Use)

These appear frequently enough to be recognizable, but aren't in every post.

### "Which is why:"
Diagnostic bridge before bullet list or evidence stack.

> "The problem isn't the tool. The problem is that your intelligence isn't structured. Which is why:
> - Every new hire needs retraining
> - Every offer launch starts from zero
> - You disappear and nothing moves"

### "Let's be crystal clear."
Sharp correction signal. Name ambiguity → state truth.

> "Let's be crystal clear. You don't have a visibility problem. You have a positioning problem."

### The Negation Ladder
Stack 2-3 "Not X" before "But Y" for escalating precision.

> "Not because you lack ideas. Not because you're not visible. But because nobody knows what you actually do."

### The Evidence Stack
List 3-4 symptoms → diagnosis implied (don't announce it).

> "Your VA re-learns your process each quarter. Your new offers take 8 weeks to launch. You can't take a week off without the business stalling. Every client onboarding needs a 90-minute call because there's nowhere to point them."

### 💋 As Period Replacement
RARE. Only for closing declarative truth-telling. Maximum 1 per piece. Instagram only.

> "Stop building on platforms that lock you in. Build where you own the architecture 💋"

### "Especially if..."
Conditional cascade. Specificity without announcement.

> "Most founders would plug in the same tool and get nothing — especially if they've never documented their decision process, their offer structure, their client history. The tool isn't the problem. The architecture is."

### Structural Inevitability Close
End with gravity, not persuasion. The thing must be true.

> "The moment you codify your genius, three things become inevitable: your business scales without you, your clients feel understood before they hire you, and you finally have time for the life you're building."

## What NOT to Sound Like

**Motivational speaker:** "YOU'VE GOT THIS!" / "Don't give up!" / "You're capable of more!"

**Tech bro:** Cold clinical. Jargon-heavy. Assumes intelligence as substitute for clarity. No philosophy.

**Guru:** Mystical without practical. Inspiration as strategy. Creates followers, not builders.

**Hustler:** "More more more faster faster faster." Optimization theater. Busy as virtue.

**Business coach:** Generic wisdom. "Proven systems." Template solutions. Could be anyone.

## Voice Check (Apply to Every Piece)

Before publishing, ask:

1. Would a management consultant say this? (If yes, it's too generic. Rewrite.)
2. Does it sound "nice" or "helpful"? (If yes, it's too soft. Rewrite.)
3. Would someone reading this think "she's describing MY business"? (If yes, keep it.)
4. Am I explaining or orienting? (Orient. Don't explain.)
5. Am I selling or stating? (State. Don't sell.)
6. Do I have minimum 3 sentence fragments? (If no, add them.)
7. Do I have at least 2 em dashes? (If no and post is 200+ words, add them.)
8. Is there at least one "Not X, but Y" or negation pattern? (If no, add it.)
9. Does the close feel like gravity or pitch? (Gravity only.)
10. Am I announcing enthusiasm where I should be demonstrating it? (Cut announcement. Show the thing.)
11. Would someone say "she's excited" or "this is true"? (They should say "this is true".)
12. Does my humor explain itself? (If yes, it's not her humor. She doesn't announce funny.)
13. What question does this implicitly answer? (Should be "what's actually true?" Not "isn't this cool?")

## Platform-Specific Adjustments

All platforms use the same voice. Adjustments are format only, not tone.

**Instagram (primary):**
- 800-1200 character captions (long-form)
- Story-weave method (moment → tension → reframe → conviction → filter CTA)
- CAPS emphasis allowed
- Typos stay (authenticity signal)
- Permission-based CTAs: "DM WORD" or nothing

**Substack (extended form):**
- 1500-3000 words
- Architectural mode primary
- Multiple sections, clear navigation
- Same voice, more breathing room
- Evidence stacks, framework reveals
- No "sign up for my newsletter" tone

**LinkedIn (strategic):**
- Shorter caps (300-600 characters)
- Same diagnostic voice, less personal storytelling
- Framework-heavy
- Professional audience filter
- Same conviction, no softening

**DMs/Stories (conversational):**
- Personal/casual mode primary
- Real language, real typos
- Shorter, punchier
- Still no softening, still precise
