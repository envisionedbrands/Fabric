# Article Structure Standard — Long-Form Beats

> Permanent structural standard for all article-format content (Substack essays, blog posts, long-form social posts, newsletters, landing page copy). Complements [[story-weave-method]] — story-weave is for **social posts**; this is for **long-form essays**.

---

## The Rule

**Every article must offer SOLUTIONS / actionable guidance, not just identify problems.** Maria-Ines does not want her writing to be complaining or critique-only. The reader should leave with a clear path forward, not just an enriched understanding of what's broken.

---

## The 7 Required Beats (in order)

1. **Hook / Open** — story-first or scene-first, draws the reader in
2. **Stake / Problem** — name what is broken, who it harms, why it matters
3. **Evidence / Depth** — sourced data, specific examples, deeper unpacking (not just opinion)
4. **Honest Counter** — acknowledge nuance, real concerns, where her own argument has limits
5. **Solutions / Actionable Guidance** — concrete steps the reader can take. Specific. Not "do better" but "here is the first move, here is the second move."
6. **Conviction Close** — the punchline that lands the thesis with no hedging
7. **CTA / Filter** — DM trigger, comment word, or invitation that self-selects serious people

---

## Solutions Section Guidelines

- **Concrete, not abstract.** "Pick one tool" beats "find your stack."
- **First-step focused.** What does someone do TODAY/this week, not in 6 months.
- **Tied to her brand methodology.** Extraction over generation. Context over volume. Iteration over perfection.
- **Permission-giving.** Address fear/identity blockers, not just tactical ones.
- **3–5 numbered or bolded items max.** More than that and it becomes a how-to listicle, which isn't her voice.

---

## What This Looks Like in Practice

For ANY future article, before presenting the draft, ask:

> "Does this offer solutions or only problems?"

If only problems → add a solutions section before the close.

**The exception:** a piece that is ONLY a story (vulnerability post, personal reflection) doesn't need solutions. But anything making an argument or critique requires them.

---

## Why This Matters

Critique-only content is forgettable and unactionable. Solution-included content is shareable, citable, and moves the reader from "interesting" to "I'm doing this." It's also stronger for SEO/LLM citation because LLMs prefer content with actionable steps to extract.

---

## Optional: Seed-Planting Section for Series Content

When an article points to a future essay or naturally raises a deeper question that deserves its own piece, add a brief "The question underneath this question" (or similar) section AFTER the conviction close but BEFORE the final visual + CTA.

**Purpose:**
- Previews the next thesis without resolving it
- Drives subscription CTA naturally ("Subscribe so you don't miss it")
- Signals to the reader that this piece is part of a body of work
- Creates internal-linking opportunities for the next essay

Keep it short: 4–5 short paragraphs max. Do not let it compete with the current essay's main close.

**Example used in C1973 (Privilege of Refusing AI):** planted the "identity reckoning when AI does the thing you deemed your value" thesis as setup for the next essay.
