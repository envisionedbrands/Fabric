# Representation Rules — Quote Covers & Non-Self Imagery

> When generating imagery that is NOT Maria-Ines herself — quote covers, editorial illustrations, crowd scenes, supporting imagery — these demographic rules apply.

---

## The Rule

When the image is not Maria-Ines, the imagery should reflect the communities the essays are written FOR and ABOUT. Substituting white models into work about underrepresented founders would undercut the argument.

---

## Demographic Distribution Target

| Group | Share of non-self imagery |
|-------|---------------------------|
| **Black women** | Strong representation |
| **Latina women** | Strong representation |
| **Asian women** | Represented |
| **Mixed-heritage / ambiguous heritage women** | Represented |
| **White women** | **~1% maximum** — rare, intentional, never default |

---

## Presentation Range (within "women" bucket)

**Welcome:**
- Short hair, shaved heads, buzz cuts
- Less traditionally feminine styling (menswear-inspired, no makeup, utilitarian)
- Range of ages (not just 20s — 30s, 40s, 50s, 60s)
- Range of body types
- Natural hair textures (coiled, coily, kinky, wavy, curly, straight — all present)
- Various skin tones within each ethnic category

**Out of scope (explicit guardrail):**
- Non-binary / gender-non-conforming / gender-fluid presentation as the subject
- Maria-Ines's call: stay within the "women" category, but broaden the presentation of womanhood

---

## When This Rule Applies

- Quote cover cards
- Editorial illustrations where a person is depicted but it's not Maria-Ines
- Article supporting imagery when the piece is about representation, gatekeeping, access, underrepresented groups
- Any generated visual that lives in the brand's public body of work

## When This Rule Does NOT Apply

- Maria-Ines's own portrait shots (she is the subject — use identity pack)
- Pure metaphor / object / architectural shots (no people)
- Client-specific deliverables where the client's demographic is the subject

---

## Why

The essays are frequently about underrepresented founders (immigrants, non-native English speakers, Global South, women of color, single parents without credentials). Using white models to illustrate these arguments would undercut the work.

Substituting one underrepresented group for another is also not honest. A Black model illustrating a Latina story is not the fix. The piece is authored by a Latina (Maria-Ines), so her face is the anchor for **her** stories. When the subject is broader — quote covers that speak to the reader, not about Maria-Ines — the demographic mix above applies.

---

## How to Apply in Prompts

When generating a non-self portrait:
- Default the demographic roll to the distribution above
- Be specific about features rather than generic ("Latina" alone triggers the stereotype the model has learned — describe skin tone, hair, structure directly)
- Same quality bar as her own portraits: warm expression, Italian Afternoon grade, no scarves, no turtlenecks, slim graceful hands if visible
- The 12-point CD checklist from [[visual-generation-rules]] still applies
