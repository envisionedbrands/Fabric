# Story-Weave Method

Content creation structure. Borrowed from Suzi Gray's storytelling mechanic, applied with Maria-Ines's voice and thesis.

This is the structural method for every piece of Envisioned Brands content that works.

---

## The Core Rule

**The thesis EMERGES from the story. Never announce thesis first and illustrate. Story → tension → reframe → conviction.**

Most founders do this wrong:
- Wrong: "Here's my insight [thesis]. Let me tell you a story that illustrates it."
- Right: "I was in the grocery line [story]. That moment taught me something [thesis emerges]."

The difference: In the wrong way, story is decoration. In the right way, story is the architecture.

---

## The 5 Steps

### Step 1: MOMENT
**Open with something that happened. A scene, conversation, memory, observation. Put reader INSIDE it before they know what the post is about.**

Don't announce the topic. Don't lead with "Listen." Don't default to a generic hook.

**Create a scene:**
- Where are they? (Location, sensory details)
- What's happening? (Action, conversation, realization)
- Who's involved? (Character, relationship)
- What's the temperature? (Energy, emotion, tone)

**Examples from Maria-Ines:**

**Example 1: Tool moment**
> "I connected a tool to my business this morning and my eyes welled up."

Reader is inside: she's in her office, she's doing something technical, she's having an unexpected emotional response. Hooked. What happens next?

**Example 2: Venezuelan lens**
> "I grew up watching systems fail. Not metaphorically. I'm Venezuelan. I watched an entire country's infrastructure collapse in real time."

Reader is inside: childhood memory, high stakes, personal history. Why is she telling us this? Keep reading.

**Example 3: Grocery line**
> "Yesterday I built a launch package for a new offer while waiting for M to get out of school. Not on a laptop. Not at a desk. From my phone, standing in the line at the supermarket."

Reader is inside: mundane moment, elevated task, ordinary location. Contradiction immediately creates curiosity.

**Example 4: Time-Rich CEO failure**
> "Last year I launched something called Time-Rich CEO and it flopped. I'm telling you this because everyone shows you the wins. Nobody shows you the moment where the market looks at your work and says 'not yet.'"

Reader is inside: vulnerability, stakes, honesty. She's not selling from a position of invincibility. She's speaking from ground level.

---

### Step 2: TENSION
**The story naturally surfaces a gap, contradiction, or question. Don't announce it ("Here's the thing..."). Let story create it.**

The reader should feel the tension before you name it.

**How tension works in the story:**
- What's contradicting?
- What's unexpected?
- What doesn't make sense yet?
- What's the gap between what should be and what is?

Going back to the grocery line example:
- **Contradiction:** Building launch packages (serious work) while standing in line (casual, distracted moment)
- **Tension:** How is she doing focused work in a chaotic space?
- **Question:** What infrastructure makes this possible?

The tension emerges from story. Reader feels it before it's named.

---

### Step 3: REFRAME
**Connect the moment to the deeper truth. Identity, legacy, AI philosophy land BECAUSE of story, not despite it.**

This is where the thesis lives. But it only lands because story has earned it.

**What reframe does:**
- Translates the specific moment into universal principle
- Connects the story to the bigger idea
- Shows why this moment matters beyond itself
- Bridges from "what happened" to "why it matters"

**Grocery line example reframe:**
> "This isn't about multitasking. It's about business designed so well you can be fully present anywhere."

The story earned this observation. It's not generic. It's specific to what happened.

**Another example (from tool moment):**
> "This isn't about tools. It's about who you become when the work changes. The tool didn't do this. I did. The reason it was powerful is because I'd already extracted my methodology."

Again: the reframe is specific to the story. It emerges from what happened.

---

### Step 4: CONVICTION
**One clear, undeniable statement. No hedging. Punchline whole post was building toward.**

This is the moment where everything lands.

**What conviction does:**
- Crystallizes the insight
- No ambiguity
- No "might," "maybe," "I think"
- One sentence that stands alone

**Examples:**

From tool story:
> "Same technology. Entirely different experience."

From time-rich-ceo story:
> "The market wasn't wrong. I was just early. Now the infrastructure exists to prove it."

From Venezuelan lens:
> "Better infrastructure beats more effort. Always."

**Critical guardrails:**
- Conviction is not a question (never end with "?")
- Conviction is not a suggestion (never say "you might consider")
- Conviction is not hedged (never say "maybe," "I think," "just")
- Conviction is not long (usually one sentence, max two)

---

### Step 5: FILTER
**Permission-based CTA that self-selects serious people. DM "WORD" or nothing at all.**

This is the final structural element. It filters for people ready to move.

**How filter works:**
- Not a question (not "What do you think?")
- Not a beg (not "Please share this")
- Not a generic CTA (not "Learn more")
- A permission request (not demand)

**Examples:**

"DM "CODIFY" if you're ready for the last AI partner you'll need."

"Comment RESET if this resonates."

"DM "INTENTIONAL" if you're building differently."

**Why filters work:**
- Self-select serious people (only aligned people respond)
- Create psychological commitment (they initiate, not you)
- Start conversation naturally (they open with the keyword)
- Build relationship on their terms

## Critical Guardrails When Writing Multiple Posts

**Guardrail 1: Never open more than 1 in 5 posts with "Listen" / "Here's the truth" / "Here's what no one explains"**

These are emergency openers. Not defaults. They're fallback positions, not signature patterns.

If you find yourself using them frequently, you're running out of stories. Go live more. Observe more. Collect more moments.

**Guardrail 2: When writing a batch, ensure each opens DIFFERENTLY**

Do not repeat the same opening structure twice:
- Don't open one post with grocery line, another with phone moment (same location, different context is fine; same structure is not)
- Don't open three posts with "I realized..."
- Don't open five posts with personal vulnerability (mix in observation, external moment, client moment)

**How to check:**
Write batch of 5-6 posts. Read just the opening lines. Do they sound different? Different energy? Different entry point?

If two sound similar, rewrite one.

**Guardrail 3: Story IS the hook, don't default to generic openers**

Wrong:
> "Listen, I've been thinking about AI and identity..."

Right:
> "I connected a tool to my business this morning and my eyes welled up."

The difference: Generic opener could belong to anyone. Story opener is irreplaceable.

## How to Use Story-Weave for Different Content Angles

The 5-step structure works across all 6 doors ([[content-strategy]]):

**Ethics/Identity Door:**
Story: Venezuela collapse. Reframe: Design beats effort. Conviction: Better infrastructure always.

**Myth-Busting Door:**
Story: Tried AI tool, disappointed. Reframe: It's not the tool. It's what you gave it. Conviction: Broken inputs = broken outputs.

**Case Study/Proof Door:**
Story: Client's journey, anonymized moment. Reframe: How infrastructure enabled the transformation. Conviction: This is what's possible.

**Philosophy Door:**
Story: Observation or conversation. Reframe: The deeper principle. Conviction: Why this matters to humanity/business/identity.

**Positioning Door:**
Story: Why Maria-Ines does what she does differently. Reframe: How her approach differs. Conviction: Why this matters.

**Movement/Vision Door:**
Story: Future moment, something that needs to change. Reframe: Why now. Conviction: Here's the call to action.

## Common Mistakes & Fixes

**Mistake 1: Thesis first, story second**
Wrong: "Here's my insight. Let me tell you a story."
Fix: "Let me tell you what happened. The insight emerges."

**Mistake 2: Story is too long**
Wrong: 500 words of setup before the point
Fix: 2-3 sentences max. Just enough to be inside it. Then move to tension.

**Mistake 3: Tension isn't clear**
Wrong: Story doesn't create question
Fix: Every story should have a natural gap. What's contradictory? What doesn't make sense? Lead with that.

**Mistake 4: Reframe is generic**
Wrong: "So here's what this taught me..." (could apply to anyone)
Fix: Specific to this story, this moment, this founder's philosophy

**Mistake 5: Conviction is hedged**
Wrong: "Maybe we should think about..." / "I kind of believe..."
Fix: One sentence. No hedging. Stands alone.

**Mistake 6: CTA is a question**
Wrong: "What do you think?"
Fix: "DM [WORD] if you're ready."

## The Rhythm & Energy

Story-Weave isn't just structure. It's rhythm.

**Short, sharp sections:**
- MOMENT: 2-3 sentences
- TENSION: Let story speak (often implicit, not stated)
- REFRAME: 1-3 sentences connecting to bigger idea
- CONVICTION: 1 sentence
- FILTER: 1 sentence

The brevity forces clarity. Every word earns its place.
