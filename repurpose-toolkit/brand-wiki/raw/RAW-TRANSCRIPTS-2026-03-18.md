## 5. Michelin Star Research (Reference Material)

To hold a Michelin star, a restaurant must consistently deliver exceptionally high-quality cuisine that meets five core, universal criteria established by the Michelin Guide. The evaluation is focused entirely on the food on the plate rather than the decor, service style, or comfort level of the establishment.

**The 5 Main Criteria for a Michelin Star:**

1. **Quality of Ingredients:** The use of fresh, high-quality, and often seasonal ingredients.
2. **Mastery of Flavor and Cooking Techniques:** The skillful preparation of food, featuring balanced, distinct flavors.
3. **The Personality of the Chef:** The cuisine must showcase the unique, creative, and individual style of the chef.
4. **Value for Money:** The dining experience must be worth the price charged.
5. **Consistency:** The restaurant must provide the same high standard of food across multiple visits, throughout the year, and across the entire menu.

**Key Requirements and Facts:**

- Stars are Awarded to the Restaurant: Michelin stars are not awarded to individual chefs, but to the establishment. If a head chef leaves, the restaurant keeps the star if the quality remains, but the inspector will re-evaluate.
- Secret, Independent Inspectors: Restaurants are visited anonymously by inspectors who pay for their meals to ensure an unbiased experience.
- Multiple Visits: Inspectors visit multiple times to ensure the quality is not a one-off experience.
- No Formal Application Necessary: While a restaurant can request to be included in the guide, there is no application process for a star. Michelin identifies potential restaurants through their own research and anonymous visits.

**Star Definitions:**

- 1 Star: "A very good restaurant in its category" (high-quality cooking, worth a stop).
- 2 Stars: "Excellent cooking, worth a detour" (skilful and carefully crafted dishes).
- 3 Stars: "Exceptional cuisine, worth a special journey" (signature dishes, precise execution, world-class).
